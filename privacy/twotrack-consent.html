Recording consent and legal note

TwoTrack records audio from your Mac and your microphone. Whether you may record a conversation depends on where you and the other participants are, on your employer's or client's policies, and on the meeting platform's terms. You are responsible for complying with them. This note is general information, not legal advice.

What the law commonly requires

  • All-party / two-party consent places (for example several US states such as California, Florida, Illinois, Pennsylvania and Washington, and many other countries) require everyone in the conversation to agree.
  • One-party consent places allow a participant to record, but recording a conversation you are not part of is still generally unlawful.
  • EU/EEA and UK (GDPR): a recording of identifiable people is personal data. Recording in a professional context needs a lawful basis, people must be informed (purpose, retention, who can access it), and recordings should be kept only as long as needed. Spain additionally protects the confidentiality of communications and a person's own voice and image.
  • Workplace rules often go further than the law: many employers and clients forbid recording without written permission.
  • If the meeting crosses borders, the strictest applicable rule is the safe default.

How TwoTrack helps

  • Consent reminder before each recording ("Have all participants agreed to recording?"), on by default.
  • Copy recording notice: one click puts this on your clipboard to paste into the meeting chat:

    I'm recording this meeting for my notes. Is everyone okay with that?

  • No stealth or automatic recording: recording only starts when you press Record; macOS shows its recording indicator, and the menu-bar icon turns red.
  • Recordings stay on your Mac (see PRIVACY.md).

Good practice

  1. Announce the recording at the start, say why, and say it stays private.
  2. Paste the notice in the chat so there is a written record of the announcement.
  3. Stop recording if anyone objects, and delete any part they ask you to.
  4. Store recordings securely and delete them when no longer needed.
  5. Don't share recordings beyond the purpose you announced.
